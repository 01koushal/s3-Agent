Diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic conditions characterized by elevated blood glucose.
Type 1 diabetes involves autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells and usually requires insulin.
Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance and/or insufficient insulin production. Risk factors can include
family history, excess body weight, physical inactivity, age, and certain metabolic conditions.
Common symptoms can include increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, blurred vision, and unexplained
weight changes. Some people with type 2 diabetes may have few or no noticeable symptoms.
Long-term high blood glucose can be associated with complications affecting the eyes, kidneys, nerves,
heart, and blood vessels. Diagnosis and treatment decisions must be made by qualified healthcare professionals.
Lifestyle measures such as balanced nutrition, regular physical activity, and weight management may support
risk reduction and management. This reference text is educational and is not a substitute for medical advice.
