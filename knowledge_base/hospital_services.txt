HOSPITAL SERVICES AND PATIENT INFORMATION

A hospital is a healthcare facility that provides assessment, diagnosis, treatment, monitoring, rehabilitation, and preventive services. Services vary by facility, and patients should check the hospital's official website or contact desk for current availability.

Emergency department: Emergency departments assess urgent and potentially life-threatening problems. Examples include severe difficulty breathing, chest pain, signs of stroke, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, major injury, or a severe allergic reaction. A person experiencing a medical emergency should contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department rather than waiting for an online answer.

Outpatient department (OPD): OPD services provide consultations without an overnight hospital stay. Patients may be asked to register, provide relevant medical history, describe symptoms and current medicines, and complete tests recommended by a qualified clinician.

Inpatient care: Admission may be recommended when a person needs continuous observation, procedures, treatment, or nursing care that cannot be safely provided as an outpatient. The treating team explains the reason for admission, planned care, and discharge criteria.

Diagnostic services: Laboratories may perform blood, urine, and other tests. Radiology services may include X-rays, ultrasound, CT, or MRI depending on the facility. A test result must be interpreted in the context of symptoms, examination, medical history, and other findings by a qualified professional.

Pharmacy: Hospital pharmacies dispense medicines according to valid prescriptions and local rules. Patients should check the medicine name, strength, instructions, expiry date, and storage requirements with the pharmacist. Do not share prescription medicines or change a prescribed dose without professional advice.

Preventive and support services: Hospitals may provide vaccination, screening, nutrition counselling, physiotherapy, mental-health support, infection prevention, and patient education. Availability differs by hospital.

Patient safety: Patients should tell clinicians about allergies, pregnancy, existing conditions, supplements, and all medicines they take. Hand hygiene, clear communication, confirming patient identity, and understanding follow-up instructions support safer care.

Appointments and records: Bring identification and relevant previous reports when attending a consultation. Ask how to access test results, referrals, prescriptions, and follow-up appointments. Protect personal health information and share it only through trusted channels.

This document provides general educational information, not a directory for a specific hospital and not medical advice. It does not establish which services are available at any particular institution.
